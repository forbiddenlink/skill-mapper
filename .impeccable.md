## Design Context

### Users
Self-directed learners mapping a craft path — developers, designers, and career switchers who want clear progression, not another generic dashboard. They open the app to see where they stand, pick the next skill, and feel momentum from mastery.

### Brand Personality
Precise · Progressive · Crafted

Emotional goals: confidence, focus, quiet satisfaction when a node flips to mastered. Never frantic neon hype or toy-like gamification.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Signal Atlas** — an architectural dark ops console for skill cartography. Near-black violet-slate canvas, precise typography (Familjen Grotesk + Instrument Sans), and a tight violet family: violet for available, lilac for mastered, indigo for in progress, pale lilac for XP/reward, with coral kept only for decay. One accent does the work of twenty neon glows. The onboarding hero is the real skill graph (all skills, all prerequisite edges), previewing what each starting role unlocks.

References: UIverse Halo (discipline), Destiny UI CSS (HUD anatomy), roadmap.sh (clarity) — re-skinned for Skill Mapper, not copied.

Anti-references: Inter/Orbitron, cyan+magenta neon, glassmorphism everywhere, purple-to-indigo gradients, glow spam, identical icon cards, “cyberpunk HUD” clichés.

Theme: dark-first product UI. Light mode is not a priority.

### Design Principles
1. **Status over spectacle** — color and motion mean locked / available / in-progress / mastered / decayed.
2. **One signal accent** — violet leads; lilac and indigo are roles, not decoration.
3. **Opaque layers beat glass** — panels are solid surfaces; blur only on modal overlays.
4. **Type does hierarchy** — display for skill titles and HUD identity; mono for XP, tiers, shortcuts.
5. **Motion for state change** — unlock, master, level-up; no decorative bounce or constant glare.
